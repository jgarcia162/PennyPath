Section 01

Condo & HOA risk

HOA & structural risk After the Surfside condominium collapse (2021), many U.S. associations faced sharper focus on reserve funding, special assessments, and lender scrutiny. Before you buy, request the reserve study and milestone inspection (where applicable) and read the minutes for assessment talk. Weak reserves can turn a “cheap” condo into a cash-flow trap overnight.
Townhouses vs. condos Townhouses often carry lower HOA fees and simpler maintenance lines than high-rise condos, which can improve cash-flow math. Always model HOA as a first-class line item — it’s often the swing factor in rental markets like yours.

    Section 05

    FHA House Hack Strategy

    12-month owner-occupancy FHA loans for 1–4 unit properties generally require you to occupy the property as your primary residence. You typically must move in within 60 days and live there at least 12 months before converting to a rental (unless an exception applies). Plan the timeline before you commit.
    House-hack math On a duplex, FHA may allow a portion of projected rent from the other unit to help qualify (often up to 75% of market rent toward income/DTI per current FHA guidelines — confirm with your loan officer and appraiser rent schedule).
    Section 06
    Verdict
    Start with FHA in your market — LLC comes later.

    FHA keeps the entry ticket small enough to match a realistic HYSA timeline. Choose a city above to tie numbers to a real market. An LLC + investment loan is often the right tool for property #2, once you have equity, documented rental history on taxes, and more dry powder for 20% down and reserves.

    Warning: HOA fees and special assessments are the single biggest risk to condo cash flow — model them conservatively every time.
